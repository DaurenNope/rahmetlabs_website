# Copy Deck — baseline + elevation notes

**Status: working draft.** The owner has flagged that the copy "needs work."
Treat `content/content.js` as the factual baseline (all claims there are
verified-safe) and **elevate the writing** — sharper, more confident, more
specific — while keeping every honesty constraint below.

## Honesty constraints (non-negotiable)

- No invented numbers, percentages, client names, testimonials, or logos.
- Usable true claims: three shipped systems are live and clickable; process
  runs 4-12 weeks from first call to running system; the first-week diagnostic
  produces a scored system plan the client keeps; the first build stage is
  fixed-price; one senior team designs, ships, and operates.
- Zero em-dashes (`—`) and no en-dash separators. Use periods, commas, colons.
- No filler verbs: elevate, seamless, unleash, next-gen, revolutionize.
- No mock-humble or performative-craftsman lines ("field notes", "quietly
  trusted by", "from the bench").
- Quotes on service cards are *customer-voice* statements (things clients say),
  not testimonials. Keep that register.

## Voice

Direct, senior, plain-spoken. Short sentences. Concrete nouns (spreadsheets,
tender portals, group chats, invoices) over abstractions. Slight confidence
edge: this team has shipped and runs what it ships. Never salesy, never cute.
Russian and Kazakh versions must read native, not translated.

## Structure and current baseline (EN)

Full strings for all three locales live in `content/content.js`. Section map:

1. **Hero** — kicker: studio identity. Headline: "Your business is drowning in
   manual work. We build the systems that end it." Subheadline: one senior
   team, replaces spreadsheets/copy-paste/follow-up threads, stays to run them.
   Primary CTA: "Tell us what's manual". Secondary: "See the work".
   *Elevation note: the headline is good; the subheadline is long. Tighten.*
2. **Thread chapters** (3-step narrative: the mess → the cost → the system).
   *Elevation note: currently abstract. Make each chapter concrete: a scene, a
   number-free cost, the turn.*
3. **What we build** — 4 failure patterns with customer-voice quotes
   ("Someone still does this by hand." etc.), each with problem + the system
   we build instead. *This is the strongest section. Keep the pattern.*
4. **Manual vs system** — comparison: same inbox, two ways to run it.
5. **Work** — ledger of shipped systems (real entries in content.js:
   Education Platform Suite, Beyond Lines, Unhireable, Multi-Platform Bot
   Suite, plus more). Live links where they exist.
6. **Process** — 4 steps with durations (Discovery 1 wk, Design 1-2 wks,
   Build & ship 2-8 wks, Operate ongoing).
7. **Principles / engagement models** — how we work, engagement shapes.
8. **FAQ** — includes the cost answer: scope sets price, written fixed-price
   estimate for a first stage after one call.
9. **Final CTA** — "Start with the manual work that eats your week." + free
   diagnostic offer + receipt line (live systems are clickable; private ones
   get a walkthrough on the call).
10. **Newsletter** — "Notes from running systems."
11. **Meta** — per-page titles/descriptions per locale. Current home title:
    "Business Automation & AI Development in Kazakhstan · Rahmet Labs".
    Keep the factual, citable style (AEO/GEO).

## Known weak spots to elevate

- Hero subheadline: 24 words, can hit harder at ~16.
- Thread chapters: abstract; ground them in recognizable scenes.
- About page: currently thin and generic; give it a point of view (why one
  accountable team beats a menu of freelancers).
- Principles: reads like every agency. Make each principle falsifiable
  (what we refuse to do).
- Footer: give it useful structure (nav, channels, languages), not decoration.
